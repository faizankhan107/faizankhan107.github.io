Research 01Time series forecasting
2025 – present

Wind power forecasting that beats persistence

Short-horizon wind power is deceptively hard: the naive persistence baseline is strong enough that most published deep models quietly lose to it. My work starts by measuring against that baseline honestly, then builds models that earn their complexity.

  • Benchmarked iTransformer, TimeXer, PatchTST, TimesNet and DLinear on ETTm1, ECL, Weather and Traffic, and on real SCADA data from a 25 MW farm at 15/30/60/180-minute horizons.
  • Designed a physics-guided Transformer with a separate physical branch and an adaptive gate, plus a regime-aware ensemble conditioned on wind-speed bin, direction sector and ramp state.
  • Built the pipeline end to end: cleaning, physical feature engineering, sliding windows over continuous segments, splits that never cross turbines or time, and bootstrap confidence intervals on every reported gain.

Research 02LLM Wiki & retrieval
2025 – present

A knowledge base that knows what it no longer believes

Document-oriented retrieval records what a document asserted, but not what that assertion replaced. Contradictions pile up unordered, and the model answers fluently from whichever chunk it happened to retrieve. I am building an LLM Wiki that keeps this straight.

  • A typed supersession relation with validity intervals on edges, so a retired fact stays traceable instead of competing with the fact that replaced it.
  • A separate cross-document resolution stage and provenance binding, with the current status projected onto compiled pages.
  • A working prototype over meeting minutes with an automated test suite, which surfaced two silent defects — per-document validation dropping cross-document edges, and traversal starting from a non-head chain member.
  • Related work on GraphRAG and multimodal retrieval: rule-based entity extraction, unified text–image nodes, and Personalized PageRank propagation over the graph.
